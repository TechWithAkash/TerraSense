# Climate assessments and restoration standards

Source excerpts: IPCC AR6 WG2, Chapter 5, Food, Fibre and Other Ecosystem Products, 2022; Society for
Ecological Restoration, International Principles and Standards for the Practice of Ecological
Restoration, 2019.

## Climate stress on agricultural systems

IPCC AR6 WG2 Chapter 5 documents that shifting rainfall patterns and rising temperatures are already
reducing yields and degrading soil condition across semi-arid agricultural regions, and identifies
nutrient runoff and pollution load as compounding, human-driven stressors that reduce ecosystem
resilience on top of climate stress itself. The chapter emphasises that interventions addressing soil
health and water retention jointly are more resilient to climate variability than single-metric fixes.

## What counts as real restoration

The SER International Principles and Standards define ecological restoration outcomes in measurable
terms rather than intent: field-margin and hedgerow restoration using native species reliably increases
pollinator visitation and measurably reduces habitat fragmentation within 1 to 2 growing seasons when
species are locally appropriate. The standards explicitly warn against counting non-native or
monoculture tree planting as restoration, since it often fails to deliver the habitat connectivity or
species richness gains that define a genuine recovery.
